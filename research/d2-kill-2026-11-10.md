# D2 kill experiment: does calibrated DP stop a learned inverter on smart-meter FL, and what does it cost on the M33? (2026-11-10)

**Verdict: D2 survives. The trade-off is not trivial on either half.**
- **Privacy/accuracy (kill rule from the D2 card).** At ε = 8 the defence-aware learned attacker (LTI) is weakened but
  not clearly defeated, and accuracy already drops by more than 1 sMAPE point. ε = 1 costs 5.4 points. So the card's
  "trivial trade-off" condition is not met: "learned attacker defeated at ε = 8 *and* accuracy loss < 1 point".
- **Cost.** DP-SGD makes the training step **4.85× more expensive** on the M33, and the noise sampler is 66% of it. That
  sampler is also unsound in float32 (see the N1 cross-link).

## Results: privacy and accuracy

Reconstruction sMAPE, mean ± SD over 16 targets (lower means the attacker does better). The prior is 0.448 ± 0.172
(observation) and 0.454 ± 0.175 (target) in every row.

| ε | σ | FL test sMAPE (Δ vs no DP) | Optimisation attack, obs / target | LTI, obs / target | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ∞ | 0 | 0.392 | 0.286 ± 0.133 / 0.287 ± 0.146 | **0.248 ± 0.109 / 0.246 ± 0.109** | Both attacks beat the prior: leakage confirmed under multi-step FedAvg, B = 4 |
| 8 | 0.451 | 0.405 (**+0.013**) | 1.416 ± 0.119 / 0.995 ± 0.147 | **0.382 ± 0.144 / 0.377 ± 0.146** | Optimisation attack defeated; LTI still ≈ 0.07 better than the prior (≈ 2 SE): not clearly defeated |
| 1 | 0.798 | 0.446 (**+0.054**) | 1.414 ± 0.122 / 1.149 ± 0.119 | **0.415 ± 0.152 / 0.413 ± 0.151** | Optimisation attack defeated; LTI within ≈ 1 SE of the prior: effectively defeated, at 5.4 sMAPE points of accuracy |

**In plain words.**
- **Without a defence, a learned attacker gets roughly halfway** from "knowing nothing" (0.45) to perfect
  reconstruction, even with 5 local steps and 4 windows per batch. That is far weaker than TS-Inverse's batch-1
  numbers, but it is real leakage.
- **ε = 8 noise blinds the optimisation attack completely.** A learned attacker trained on noisy updates still extracts
  a little. Accuracy loss is about 1.3 points.
- **ε = 1 stops both attackers** (the learned one is within noise of the prior), but forecast error rises by about
  5 points. So the privacy that actually works is not cheap in accuracy, and on the chip it costs 4.85× per step.

## Results: cost on the Cortex-M33 (MEASURED, QEMU instruction counts; cycles not calibrated)

Code: `experiments/d2-sensor-gradinv/mcu/dpstep.c`, the same FCN in float32, B = 4, the D3 harness.

| Window | Instructions | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Plain training step (4 × forward/backward + SGD update) | **1.16 M** | |
| DP-SGD step (per-example backward into a buffer, L2 clip, accumulate, Gaussian noise, update) | **5.63 M (4.85×)** | |
| Noise only (16,608 Gaussian samples: ChaCha20 + float32 Box-Muller) | **3.72 M (66% of the DP step)** | RFC 8439 ChaCha20 KAT passes |
| Clip only (one per-example gradient) | 0.17 M | ×4 per step |
| Extra RAM for DP | +66 kB (one per-example gradient buffer): about 199 kB static, against 256 kB on the EFR32MG24 | arm-none-eabi-size |

**Per FL round (5 local steps), energy estimate** (CPI 1–2 × 0.100–0.147 nJ/cycle, radio 0.50 µJ/B on air; ASSUMPTION
constants from the datasheet table in `direction-mining-2026-11-05.md`):
- Compute: plain step 0.58–1.71 mJ; DP step **2.8–8.3 mJ**.
- Radio: uploading 66 kB of float32 update takes 652 frames, about 86.6 kB on air, about **43 mJ**.
- So DP adds about 5–15% to a round's energy once the radio is counted. That is small next to the radio, but 4.85× on
  the CPU, and it nearly fills RAM.
- With a *sound* constant-time sampler (N1 estimate: 46–98 M instructions per step), compute would grow to about
  23–72 mJ per round. At that point it rivals the radio.

## Cross-link to N1 (`direction-mining-niche-2026-11-10.md`)

The noise this cost table pays for is float32 Box-Muller from 24-bit uniforms. In a single-step (FedSGD-style) release,
a floating-point pair-inversion attack running on the same M33 code path decides membership of a worst-case canary
from one released pair with about 96% accuracy (MEASURED). An ideal Gaussian at the same σ allows 73–87%.

Whether that attack survives D2's 5-step FedAvg upload is untested. It is N1's week-1 kill experiment. The 5.77σ tail
cut alone inflates δ only from 10⁻⁵ to 4.8×10⁻⁴ at ε = 8 (exact accounting).

## Limitations

- **Reduced attackers.** No full TS-Inverse and a reduced LTI, so the leakage numbers are a lower bound on a strong
  attacker.
- **Narrow attack setting.** The attack is at round 0 only, and only 16 targets (SDs are wide).
- **Stricter threshold.** The "defeated = no better than prior" rule is stricter than a usefulness threshold on load
  curves.
- **Uncalibrated M33 cost.** The instruction counts are not calibrated against real M33 cycles.
- **One dataset, one model.**
